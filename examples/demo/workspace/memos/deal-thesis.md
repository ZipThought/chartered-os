# Project Citadel — Deal Thesis (v0.4)

**Author:** Vesper Capital deal team
**Date:** 2026-04-22
**Status:** Working draft for IC pre-read

## Thesis

Stronghold Networks is a category-leading enterprise security software business
whose growth has been masked by a heavy services tail. We believe a
software-first repositioning, a rationalized go-to-market motion, and selective
product investment in the threat-intel module create a path to a 3.5x MOIC
over a four-year hold.

The opportunity exists because the founder-CEO has resisted services
divestiture and the existing PE sponsor is fund-life-constrained. Our
operating thesis assumes we can move services to a managed-partner model
within twelve months of close.

## Key value drivers

1. **Services to MSP partners.** We expect to redirect roughly $58M of services
   revenue to a managed-services partner network within twelve months,
   freeing implementation capacity and improving software-only gross margin.
2. **Pricing renormalization.** Current tier-pricing has not been adjusted
   in three years; benchmarking against four nearest comparables suggests
   a defensible 18-24% list price increase at the top tier.
3. **Threat-intel module as upsell vector.** The module is the most rapidly
   growing component of the platform but is sold reactively rather than as
   the lead motion.

## Risks (preliminary)

The largest unresolved risks remain customer concentration (top three
customers represent 31% of ARR) and key-person exposure on the engineering
side. Diligence Stewards are tasked with surfacing change-of-control
exposure across the top-twenty customer contracts and validating the
employment, retention, and non-compete posture of the senior technical
staff.

## Open questions for diligence

- Whether the change-of-control language across the top-twenty customer
  contracts permits the contemplated transaction without renegotiation.
- Whether the CTO retention package survives the planned R&D consolidation
  under Vesper's existing portfolio CTO.
- Whether the asset purchase agreement's indemnity cap and MAC clause
  protect the buyer against the surfaced concentration and key-person
  risks.
