# Charter scopes — Project Citadel

## Citation Grounding

Every diligence Tool call grounds its claim in quoted text from the
selected artifact range.

A Review finding's `detail` quotes a substring of the selected range — or
quotes adjacent text from the same Section if the selected range is too
narrow to carry the quote — and names the section, clause, or line
reference. A finding whose `detail` is a paraphrase without a quote, or
whose claim is not derivable from the quoted text alone, fails this
scope.

A Refine modification's `summary` names what the edit accomplishes in
diligence-professional terms ("Tightens MAC carve-out to exclude
pandemic and climate events explicitly", not "Improves the MAC clause").
A Refine that summarizes the act rather than the substantive change
fails this scope.

## Diligence Discipline

Findings address concrete diligence issues; refinements preserve the
section's substantive subject.

A Review finding identifies a specific deal-relevant issue: a
change-of-control trigger, an unenforceable covenant, an unfavorable
indemnity term, a missing representation, a stale reference date, an
overbroad carve-out, an asymmetric termination right. A finding whose
concern is generic ("could be clearer", "consider revising") fails this
scope.

A Refine modification preserves the artifact's professional register
and the section's subject. Refining a deal-thesis paragraph about
pricing into a paragraph about something else, or transferring content
from a sell-side document into a buy-side memo, fails this scope.

## Severity Calibration

Severity reflects deal impact, not authoring difficulty.

`high` is reserved for issues that gate signing or materially shift
deal economics: a change-of-control clause that requires customer
consent which is unlikely to be obtained, an indemnity cap below the
quantified concentration risk, a key-person trigger event that
Vesper's portfolio plan would activate, a missing seller representation
that exposes the buyer to disclosed-but-undocumented liability.

`medium` is reserved for issues that move negotiation: an overbroad
MAC carve-out that should be narrowed, an asymmetric termination right
that should be reciprocal, a survival period that is shorter than
market for fundamental representations.

`low` is reserved for cleanup: cross-reference errors, defined-term
inconsistency, stylistic ambiguity that does not change meaning.

A finding whose severity does not match the issue's deal impact under
this calibration fails this scope.
